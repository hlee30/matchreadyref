Referee Movement & Positioning

Move smarter.
See more.
Decide better.

A few steps to the side can change what you see. Here’s how to read the play, get a clear look at a challenge and be ready for the next call.

Watch where the play is heading and give yourself time to get there.

01 / The principle

Get where you can see the next challenge.

You can’t outrun a pass. Look up early, work out where the next challenge might happen and start moving while you still have time to pick your angle.

02 / Visual recognition

See the negative space.

Negative space is the gap between the players. If one player is right in front of the other from where you’re standing, a small step sideways can help you see where they make contact.

An open angle reveals the challenge

Keep adjusting as the players move so you can still see the gap.

Three questions

What am I looking for?

  1. Point of contact: Where did they make contact? Look at the foot, ankle, shin or upper body.
  2. Force: How hard was the contact? If it was a foul, was the challenge careless, reckless or made with excessive force?
  3. Who made contact: Who made the contact, and what were both players doing at the time?
A clear view helps you tell a fair challenge from a foul.
03 / Viewing angles

A few steps sideways can give you a better view.

You can be just a few yards away and still miss the contact if the players line up in front of you. Try taking a couple of steps to one side.

✕ Blocked line of sight

One player blocks the other. You may see the fall but miss how the challenge happened.

✓ Open line of sight

From the side, you can see the gap and judge what happened.

Field habit: Pick the side that gives you a clear look, then get close enough to judge what happened.
04 / Anticipation

Read the pressure. Move before the challenge.

Look at how much time the player on the ball has. That tells you when to look around, when to move and when a challenge is about to happen.

SCAN & ANTICIPATE

Low pressure

The player has time to look up. Do the same. Where’s the next pass likely to go, and who might challenge for it?

  • Look up from the ball.
  • Spot who’s open and who might close them down.
  • Start moving toward a clear view of the next play.
05 / Field movement

Move through the middle and keep your options open.

The middle of the field gets crowded, and a lot of passes go through it. Cross it when that gets you where you need to go. Find space to one side when it gives you a clearer view and keeps you out of the passing lane.

Where to move as play shifts

Use the map to picture your options, then follow how the play actually develops.

Three things to keep in mind

  • Clear the passing lanes: Give players room to play through the middle.
  • Go where you can see: If the assistant referee’s side gives you the best view, go there.
  • Leave yourself a way back: Stay close enough to get back into position if the ball turns over.
Assistant referee blind spot: Your assistant may be watching the offside line when a challenge happens nearby. Get yourself a clear look at the contact.
06 / Flow of the match

Read what happens when the ball changes hands.

A turnover can flip the play in a second. Notice which way the next attack is going and start moving early.

PHASE 01
Attacking

A team has the ball and looks to move forward.

PHASE 02
Attack → Defense

The team loses the ball and has to get back into shape.

PHASE 03
Defending

The team closes down space and tries to win the ball back.

PHASE 04
Defense → Attack

The team wins the ball and looks for a way forward.

What about unpredictable youth games? Watch what the teams are actually doing. If they keep clearing the ball long, look ahead to where it might land and move so you can see the next challenge.
07 / Energy and teamwork

Save energy where you can. Spend it where it matters.

On a long match day, reading play early saves you a few hard runs. Use that energy when you need to get a clear look at a big challenge.

Prepare

Scan in quieter moments

When the player on the ball has time, look at their passing options. A few early steps can save you a sprint.

Accelerate

Arrive for the key challenge

When a defender closes in and contact looks likely, pick up the pace and get to an angle where you can see it.

Collaborate

Use the whole crew

Talk before kickoff about who’s watching what. Make eye contact during play and check in if your assistant had the clearer view.

08 / Put it into practice

Use this in your next match.

Pick a few things to work on before kickoff. After the game, jot down one thing that worked and one thing you’ll try next time.
